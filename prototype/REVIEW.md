# Homepage evaluation and proposed direction

Based on all 12 uploaded desktop screenshots, plus the public homepage HTML. This is a visual and content review, not a functional audit of production. No mobile capture was supplied.

| Priority | Observed weakness | Proposed improvement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High | Repeated oversized uppercase headings compete with product photography. Supporting copy and card actions are comparatively small. | Expressive sentence-case display headings, readable UI text, and a quieter heading scale below the hero. |
| High | Personalized gifts are stated, but Live Preview and AI Preview are not explained in the opening journey. | Introduce the two existing capabilities after the first product selection, with product-compatibility wording. No new preview workflow. |
| High | Many full-width green product buttons compete with the hero CTA. | One primary shopping action per section; make entire product cards clear, keyboard-accessible links with a restrained text action. |
| Medium | Category layout is four cards plus one orphan, leaving a large blank area. | Five columns on wide screens and a deliberate mobile arrangement. |
| Medium | Very large vertical gaps separate sections while the product rail is dense. | Use a consistent container and spacing scale, alternating compact shopping sections with quieter editorial sections. |
| Medium | Carousel depends on swipe guidance and a native horizontal scrollbar. | Add visible previous/next controls, keyboard access and mobile scroll snapping. |
| Medium | Review text is sparse and repeated. | Preserve the supplied words and authors, without inventing more persuasive testimonials, dates or verification labels. |
| Low | FAQ numbering skips 4; repeated labels add noise. | Remove unnecessary numbering and use native, accessible disclosures. Preserve the published answers. |
| Medium | Generic social root links appear in the live HTML. | Keep social destinations visibly out of scope in the prototype; do not imply these are merchant profiles. |

Strengths worth retaining: warm lifestyle photography, a recognizable green merchant color, actual personalization examples, visible product prices, and a complete homepage journey including help and contact.

## Recommended direction
An editorial gift shop: warm cream, deep green, a distinctive serif for display text, and a precise sans-serif for shopping information. Keep images rectangular and generous, let products provide the color, and use fine rules instead of boxed panels. The merchant's identity remains configurable rather than turning every store into Customelo marketing.

First viewport: a calm merchant header, a large two-line “Personalized gifts.” heading, the original supporting copy, one shopping CTA, and warm product imagery. The primary route is hero → featured products → explanation of existing preview capabilities → categories/catalog. A wallet feature, unchanged customer quotes, FAQs and contact complete the journey.

Signature interaction: the catalog rail advances by a visible card group and snaps on touch. Motion is confined to this browsing action and small affordance changes, with reduced-motion support.

Honest limits: source screenshots limit product-image resolution. SVG viewport crops preserve the supplied images without regenerating or inventing products. Mobile behavior is a proposed responsive composition, not a verified reproduction of production. Menu/category relationships and catalog slices are illustrative presentation data, labeled in prototype controls. Merchant configuration and preview generation remain in the real application.
